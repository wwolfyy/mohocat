# R-0436 — 139 record titles trailed off mid-phrase — the import used an item first physical line as its title

> **task · done** · 2026-08-09
> Found while repairing `R-0435`, by reading the ten repaired titles back.

---

**Symptom.** `R-0240`'s title is _"B2 c2fc78f — config-driven, per-mountain playlist filing.
social"_. `R-0186`'s ends on a comma. `R-0198`'s ends on _"the"_. Forty-five titles across the
store end mid-phrase, and **44 of them come from `PROJECT_PLAN.md`**.

**Root cause.** The importer took each checkbox item's **first physical line** as its title. Line
breaks in that file land wherever prettier's 100-column wrap put them, so a title ends on whatever
word happened to sit at column 100 — _"Fixed:"_, _"Investigation showed the"_, _"but NOT the way"_.

> ⚠️ **The sentence above blames the wrong thing. See "Correction" at the end of this record.**
> Prettier never wrapped that prose. The rest of the paragraph is right.

⚠️ **This is a different defect from `R-0435`, despite the shared root of "the importer trusted
line boundaries".** `R-0435` was **loss** — prose that existed nowhere else. This is not loss: the
full text is in the record body, one line below. It is a **legibility** cost, and it lands on the
surface that matters most, because a title is the only thing `checkout.js --query` and
`registry.md` show. A title that ends in _"and"_ costs a record open on every scan of the store.

**Why it is its own piece of work.** Forty-five titles is forty-five judgment calls — a title has
to say what the item _is_, which the first line frequently does not, so it cannot be scripted. It
also touches only the `title` field, so it is a clean pass: check out the 45, rewrite, check in.

📌 **Detection.** A title ending on `,` `:` `&` or a function word:

```bash
node work_tracking/scripts/checkout.js --query "title LIKE '%,' OR title LIKE '%:' OR title LIKE '% the' OR title LIKE '% a' OR title LIKE '% and'"
```

📌 **Worth deciding first, and it is an owner call:** whether titles are allowed to be **rewritten**
at all, or whether they must stay verbatim-from-source the way record bodies do. `.prettierignore`
argues bodies are verbatim so `source_ref` stays checkable against `git show`. A title is not a
quotation and has no such contract — but the migration has not said so anywhere.

---

## Resolution (2026-08-09)

✅ **Owner's answer: a title may be rewritten.** A title is a label, not a quotation, and no
`source_ref` contract rests on it. **Record bodies stay verbatim**, so `git show` still checks out
against the pinned commit — only the `title` field moved.

⚠️ **The count in this record was wrong, and low. It is 139, not 45.** The `LIKE` query above only
catches a title ending on `,` `:` `&` or a function word, and a line wrap lands wherever column 100
falls — frequently mid-noun. `R-0240` is the case that shows it: it ends on _"social"_, a perfectly
ordinary word, and is nonetheless cut mid-identifier (the body continues `.youtubePlaylistId`).

🔑 **The reliable detector is structural, not lexical.** The importer took the item's **first
physical line**, so the defect is visible by comparing a record's title against the first line of
its own body — markdown stripped, whitespace collapsed. Where the two are equal and the body's
second line continues the same block, the title is a wrap:

- **157** records had their title taken from a wrapped first line.
- **18** of those still end at a sentence boundary and were **left alone** — a full sentence is a
  legitimate title even if the body says more.
- **139** ended mid-phrase and were rewritten.

**What each new title is.** The item's **first complete sentence** wherever that reads as a
description, extended by a clause where the first sentence was too thin to identify the record
(`Q4 — UI.` became `Q4 — UI: 삭제 beside the existing 수정 in PostList, and inline 수정 + 삭제 on a
reply's own card`). Every plan-letter prefix (`B2`, `D1e`, `P8`, `U7`) is kept, because
`PROJECT_PLAN.md`'s sections address the items by those letters.

📌 **Four parents took a revision they did not need**, and the store was right to insist: `R-0154`,
`R-0169` and `R-0343` are `split_from` targets of records whose titles changed, and `checkin`
refuses to write a child without appending to its parent. Their notes say exactly that, so the
timelines do not read as silent edits.

🔑 **A gate already covered the second half of this.** `store.test.js` asserts every record file
names its own record in the H1, so rewriting 139 titles without touching the 139 files failed
immediately — the `title` field and the record file cannot drift apart unnoticed.

---

## Correction (2026-08-12) — Prettier did not create those line breaks

**What this record claimed.** That the line breaks the importer tripped over "land wherever
prettier's 100-column wrap put them".

**Why that is wrong.** Prettier only rewraps prose when its `proseWrap` option is set to `always`.
The default is `preserve`, which leaves existing line breaks exactly as written. This repository's
`.prettierrc` sets `printWidth` to 100 but has never set `proseWrap`, and there is no other Prettier
configuration file. So Prettier has never reflowed a paragraph here, and it did not produce the
wrapping in `PROJECT_PLAN.md`.

**What actually produced it.** Whoever wrote the file wrapped it by hand at roughly 100 columns to
match `printWidth`. That is why the breaks look machine-made and fall near a consistent column.

**Nothing about the defect or the fix changes.** The importer took each item's first physical line,
and it made no difference what created that line. All 139 titles were still truncated, and they are
still fixed.

**Why the correction is worth making anyway.** A reader who believes Prettier caused this has an
obvious remedy available that would accomplish nothing: setting `proseWrap`, or excluding
`PROJECT_PLAN.md` from formatting. Both would leave the real cause untouched, because the real cause
was an importer that treated a line break as a semantic boundary.

📌 **`R-0438` is the same shape and is the precedent.** That record withdrew a measured-sounding
figure — "643 of 1,274 matching rows" — after it turned out to come from an experiment nobody could
reproduce. In both cases the conclusion survived and the evidence offered for it did not.
