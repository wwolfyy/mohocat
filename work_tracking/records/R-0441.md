# R-0441 — SCHEMA.md was two documents wearing one name; the workflow is now WORKFLOW.md

> **decision · done · adopted** · 2026-08-10
> Owner-raised while reviewing where `R-0440`'s two guards had been filed.

---

**The question was narrow and the answer was not.** `R-0440` put two guards in `SCHEMA.md` §3 and a
short form in `AGENTS.md`. The owner asked whether they should be in `AGENTS.md` §A instead.
Measuring it turned up the larger fact: **`SCHEMA.md` §3 ran 144 lines of a 439-line file**, so
roughly a third of a document whose own header says _"`schema.sql` is normative; this file explains
it"_ was not explaining the schema at all. It was the check-out loop, looking things up, `work.json`
and its stamp, merge-conflict recovery, and never-grep.

🔑 **The split is by _kind_, and the kind that matters is whether a reader must have seen it.**

| Kind         | Question it answers                              | Where it goes  | Read           |
| ------------ | ------------------------------------------------ | -------------- | -------------- |
| **Guard**    | what must I not get wrong?                       | `AGENTS.md` §A | unconditional  |
| **Workflow** | how do I operate the store?                      | `WORKFLOW.md`  | before first use |
| **Schema**   | what is a field / relationship / view?           | `SCHEMA.md`    | on demand      |

⚠️ **`AGENTS.md` loads every session; `SCHEMA.md` is introduced as _"read it before your first
check-in"_.** A guard exists to stop a mistake before it happens, so a guard living only in the
opt-in file prevents nothing for a session that never opens it. That asymmetry — not length — is
why the guards moved.

📌 **The inconsistency this exposed, and the reason it was worth acting on:** `AGENTS.md` already
carried never-grep almost in full, worked example included, while giving never-hand-edit-the-store
one clause — even though hand-editing the store is by far the more damaging of the two. The guards
now carry their _reasoning_ in `AGENTS.md`, because the reasoning is what makes a rule stick.

## What was rejected

❌ **Folding all 144 lines into `AGENTS.md` §A.** It would have taken §A from 66 lines to ~210 and
turned the auto-loaded orientation into a manual — the same "nobody reads it in one sitting" failure
that `HANDOFF.md` hit at 3,396 lines, just relocated. Orientation and manual are different jobs.

❌ **Renumbering `SCHEMA.md`'s sections after the removal.** `§4`, `§5`, `§6` and `§7` are cited by
number from records (`R-0408`, `R-0421`, `R-0422`), from `BACKLOG.md` and from `checkin.js`. Record
prose is **verbatim** from its source so `source_ref` stays checkable against `git show` —
renumbering would either break the citations or force rewriting text that is pinned to a commit.
✅ **`§3` therefore stays, as a pointer**, and every later number is untouched.

## What moved, and what followed it

- **`WORKFLOW.md`** (new, 179 lines): the loop and its commands, the two `R-0440` guards with their
  mechanism, looking things up, `work.json` and its stamp, merge-conflict recovery, never-grep.
- **`SCHEMA.md`**: 439 → 312 lines. §3 is now four paragraphs pointing at `WORKFLOW.md` and saying
  why the number stayed.
- **`AGENTS.md` §A**: both guards in full, and the pointer list now names `WORKFLOW.md` (how) and
  `SCHEMA.md` (what) as separate things.
- **Six inbound `§3` citations repointed** — `checkout.js`, `checkin.js`, `build.js` and `lib.js`
  header comments, the `/handoff` skill, and two in the restructure doc. ⚠️ **`link-check.js` could
  not have caught any of them**: they are section references in prose, not markdown links.
- **`size-policy.json`**: `WORKFLOW.md` added at 250 lines, and **`SCHEMA.md` ratcheted 450 → 350**.
  🔑 Lowering it matters more than adding the new one — a budget left at 450 against a 312-line file
  would have silently licensed back exactly the 138 lines just removed.

📌 **`R-0440` is superseded in substance but not replaced**, so it keeps its id and takes `rev 2`
rather than being marked dead: its finding — that neither guard was written down — is still true and
still the reason both exist. Only the location changed. That revision was written as a **new
revision with a `note`**, which is the rule `R-0440` itself introduced.

## `rev 2` (2026-08-10) — the split copied instead of splitting

⚠️ **The first execution of this decision duplicated both guards rather than placing them.** They
were written out in full in `AGENTS.md` §A **and** in `WORKFLOW.md` §2. The owner caught it.

🔑 **`WORKFLOW.md`'s own header already stated the correct rule and the file broke it 40 lines
later** — the header says _"What is here is the mechanism behind them; what is there is the rule"_,
and §2 then restated both rules verbatim. Both were written in the same edit, which is how easy
this is to do while believing you are doing the opposite.

**The layering, now applied — and it is the same by-kind cut, one level down:**

| Piece of a rule       | Where          | Because                                                     |
| --------------------- | -------------- | ----------------------------------------------------------- |
| the instruction       | `AGENTS.md` §A | it must be read before the mistake, not after                |
| a one-clause _why_    | `AGENTS.md` §A | a bare imperative does not stick                             |
| the evidence in full  | `WORKFLOW.md`  | it does not fit in a file every session loads unconditionally |

⚠️ **`AGENTS.md` now carries an explicit "do not restate it here"**, because the next person to
improve the guards will otherwise improve them by expanding them, which is exactly how the copy
came back. **`WORKFLOW.md` §2 was reworded from instruction to explanation** — its headings are now
_"What a hand-written line skips"_ and _"Why the prose has to be finished first"_, so a reader who
lands there gets the reasoning and is told the rule lives elsewhere.

📌 **Why `rev 2` and not a new record.** The decision has not changed — it is still _split by kind_.
What changed is that the execution did not honour it. A second record would fragment one decision
across two ids, and `SCHEMA.md` §5's test applies: same item, later state, so same `id` and a higher
`rev`.

⚠️ **Three older duplications between the two files survive this** and are `R-0442`, deliberately
not folded in here: fixing them means editing text this decision did not write, and one of them is
a genuine judgement call rather than a slip.
