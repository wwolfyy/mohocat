## The decision, and how it changed

**As taken on 2026-08-13:** apply marker scarcity to the four documents every session reads —
`AGENTS.md`, `WORKFLOW.md`, `SCHEMA.md` and `HANDOFF.md` — and enforce it with a gate rather than
a written convention.

**Amended by the owner the same day, when the executing session put the gate's two open questions
to them.** The gate is not to be built:

> "I don't want to be too strict about this. Let's not gate this. Let's make this a guideline — be
> judicious without being stingy. That's a vague standard, but this sort of decision is what AI
> agents are for."

That answer also made the second question moot, since counting rules only matter to a threshold.
**The scarcity half of the decision stood and was applied; only the enforcement half was dropped.**

Records are explicitly out of scope. There are more than 440 of them, they are read on demand
rather than every session, and the prose of every imported one is verbatim from its source with
`source_ref` pinning it to a commit. Rewriting those would break the audit trail the migration
rests on. New records should simply be written lighter as a habit.

Two things follow from this that were not obvious before measuring. The density is the
repository's existing convention rather than something the 2026-08-10 documents introduced, and
`HANDOFF.md` is the densest file in the repository while also being the one `AGENTS.md` instructs
every session to read first.

**Re-measured at execution time, `HANDOFF.md` had grown denser still** — 226 lines carrying 46
markers, one per 4.9 — because the intervening session had added state to it without removing any.

## Why it is a problem

A signalling system works by being scarce. The repository assigns these markers real meanings: the
warning triangle means watch out, the key means this is the load-bearing point, the pin means this
is an aside. At one marker every five to seven lines, close to every paragraph carries one, so the
markers no longer tell a reader which paragraph to slow down for.

There is a second effect that matters more, and it is why this is a writing problem rather than a
formatting preference. Writing "key: X" in bold asserts that X is important. Writing "X matters
because Y" earns it. Reaching for a marker is one of the ways the "because" gets dropped, so the
density is a symptom of the same habit `R-0447` corrected in seven specific places.

Bold has a third problem of its own. It is doing three unrelated jobs in one visual channel:
ordinary emphasis, marking key terms such as `row` and `rev`, and acting as a headline label at the
start of a paragraph. A reader who scans the bold gets a broken telegram rather than a summary.

## The gate that was proposed, and why it was declined

The argument for a gate was `R-0424`: the archive mechanism for `HANDOFF.md` was never missing, it
simply stopped being applied, until the size budget made it a CI failure. A density convention
would decay the same way, and it would decay invisibly, because nothing about a too-marked document
fails.

Two questions had to be answered before any gate could be built, and putting them to the owner is
what produced the reversal:

- **What ratio counts as scarce?** `SCHEMA.md` at one per 17.3 read comfortably and was the
  candidate. Setting the ceiling at the current worst value would have gated nothing.
- **Does the ceiling count distinct markers or total markers?** The two rules disagreed most on
  `AGENTS.md`, which carried 33 markers across only 18 paragraphs: a per-marker rule scored it 9.7
  and failed it, a per-paragraph rule scored it 17.8 and passed it.

The owner's answer was that this is a judgement call worth leaving to judgement. **The `R-0424`
precedent is therefore not universal** — it applies to a rule with a crisp threshold, such as a line
count, and the owner drew the line at rules where picking the threshold is itself the hard part.
Nothing was added to `size-check.js` or `size-policy.json`.

## What was actually changed

| Document      | Before          | After            |
| ------------- | --------------- | ---------------- |
| `HANDOFF.md`  | 46, one per 4.9 | 16, one per 12.7 |
| `AGENTS.md`   | 33, one per 9.7 | 15, one per 21.4 |
| `WORKFLOW.md` | 26, one per 9.1 | 10, one per 23.6 |
| `SCHEMA.md`   | 18, one per 17.3 | 14, one per 22.3 |

The vocabulary narrowed as much as the count. `HANDOFF.md` had used twelve different marker kinds
and now uses three: ⚠️ for something that will bite the reader, 📌 for a genuine aside, and 🔴 for
the two items most urgently needing a person. What was removed was almost entirely markers standing
at the head of ordinary prose — the `▶️`, `📊`, `🎉`, `📁` and `📏` headline labels, and the
`🔁 🕐 🔍 📝` badges that labelled the R1–R4 rules in `AGENTS.md` and their matching sections in
`WORKFLOW.md`, where the rule's own number already said which rule it was.

Two kinds of marker were deliberately kept even though they are frequent. The five `❌` bullets in
the anti-patterns list in `AGENTS.md` are doing bullet duty rather than signalling, and the `✅`/`❌`
pair in the `WORKFLOW.md` §2.2 table is the table's own yes/no column. Stripping either would have
been the stinginess the owner warned against.

`HANDOFF.md` remains the densest of the four at one per 12.7. That is deliberate: it is the file
whose content is disproportionately hazards and state, so a higher rate of genuine warnings is
correct there.

## Corrected in the same pass

Rewriting `HANDOFF.md` surfaced statements that had rotted since it was last written, and leaving
known-false text in a file being edited for readability would have been perverse:

- It claimed **five files were uncommitted**; they had landed as `a779cc8`, and the tree was clean
  apart from the two untracked `code-graph-tooling-*` files.
- It gave **open work as 53 items (45 tasks)**; the true figure was 46 items and 38 tasks.
- It named `R-0447.md` and `R-0448.md` as **untracked but belonging to the change**, which stopped
  being true when they were committed.
- Its own **"densest file in the repository at one marker every 5.1 lines"** aside was made false by
  this very change, and was removed rather than updated.

The `R-0448` entry in the "Start here" order was also removed, since this record is what closes it,
leaving `R-0449` first.

## Where the guideline now lives

`HANDOFF.md` carries it, in the section on its own size budget: be judicious without being stingy,
reserve ⚠️ for what will bite the reader and 📌 for a genuine aside, roughly one per fifteen lines
as a shape rather than a number to hit. It is stated once, in one file, per the rule against a
second copy that drifts.
