## What the pass found

✅ **Done 2026-08-09. Fourteen candidates read; five imported, nine dropped as already covered.**
All three pointer rows (`R-0335`, `R-0339`, `R-0346`) carry a `note` at `rev: 2` saying what was
lifted and what was left, so the next reader does not repeat the probe.

**Imported** — each because its _reasoning_ was in no row, even where the conclusion was:

- **`R-0430`** — management only, not custody, and why. `R-0400` had the answer as one item in a
  Q1–Q8 summary; the trade-off that produced it (who is PIPA controller, whose bill, what happens
  to them if you stop) was nowhere, nor was the counter-consideration that would reverse it.
- **`R-0431`** — B1 makes isolation a correctness property, and the shared permission check had
  no mountain dimension. The sharpest finding in the framework: `contacts` PII was readable by
  any `manage-users` holder, so scoping B1 as a per-collection sweep would have shipped a leak.
- **`R-0432`** — GA4 stays one shared property; dual-tagging deferred. The reason is that GA4
  permissions are property-level, so a second owner granted access sees geyang's numbers.
- **`R-0433`** — the login-friction fix rejected, because both designs mean owning a bearer
  credential Firebase does not validate.
- **`R-0434`** — path-based is not a one-way door; the middleware and `domains` stay.

**Dropped, with the record that already holds it:** the Supabase set-aside (`R-0399`), Q1–Q8
(`R-0400`), the path-based decision itself and the hostname/apex/sequencing answers (`R-0395`),
session propagation (`R-0396`), the sign-out defect (`R-0379`, `R-0384`), Kakao's redirect URI
(`R-0397`), and the analytics decoupling (`R-0091`, `R-0088`).

document — decisions settled inside task rows are invisible to the query that should surface
them.
