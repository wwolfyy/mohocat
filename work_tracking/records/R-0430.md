# R-0430 — Management only, not custody — the second mountain owner manages content; you hold the data, the bill and the PIPA duty

> **decision · done · adopted** · 2026-07-19 · §9
> Lifted from `docs/planning/completed/multi-tenant-architecture-decision-20260718.md` §3
> (`docs/planning/completed/multi-tenant-architecture-decision-20260718.md#3@b77e5af`).

---

**The framework called this "the gating question", and it is a business question rather than a
technical one:**

> Does the second mountain's owner need to _hold_ their data — their own billing, their own
> console, the ability to leave with it — or do they need to _manage content_ through the admin
> CMS, scoped to their mountain?

**Answered "management only" by the owner on 2026-07-19** (Q1). Everything downstream branched on
it: B1 (one Firestore with `mountainId` scoping) and A1 (one Vercel project) are only coherent
under management-only, and custody would have forced a separate Firebase project per mountain.

📌 **`R-0400` already records the answer** as one item in the Q1–Q8 summary. This row exists for
the reasoning, which was nowhere in the store.

## What the answer actually buys and costs

| | **Custody** | **Management only** (chosen) |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Means | A Firebase project per mountain | Shared project, RBAC + `mountainId` scoping |
| Data isolation | Free — a different database | **Enforced by rules and query scoping** |
| Who is the PIPA controller | Each owner, for their users | **You, for everyone** |
| Whose bill | Theirs | **Yours** |
| If you stop maintaining it | They continue independently | **They lose the platform** |
| Central auth (requirement 1) | Awkward — needs a two-backend split | Natural |
| Central analytics (requirement 3) | Awkward — cross-property reporting | Natural |
| Existing RBAC reusable | Partly | **Yes — already modelled per mountain** |

🔑 **The three stated owner requirements all pointed the same way.** Central auth (so a second
owner does not have to stand up Kakao OIDC, SMS and OAuth consent themselves), localized content
management, and central analytics with a per-mountain breakdown — each is natural under a shared
backend and awkward under custody. Requirement 2's wording, _"management of cats, feeding spots,
media… handled by them"_, is what the `currentRole.mountainId` model already anticipated.

⚠️ **The counter-consideration, which is the thing that would reverse this.** The original
motivation was that _"the owner of that mountain may be different"_. If that owner is an
independent party rather than a delegate, custody may matter for reasons that have nothing to do
with the code — liability, trust, continuity, or simply their own wish not to depend on you.
Management-only makes you the PIPA controller for their users and the single point of failure for
their site.

📌 **Consequence worth keeping in view:** under this answer, isolation stopped being structural
and became a correctness property that the code has to maintain — see `R-0431`.
